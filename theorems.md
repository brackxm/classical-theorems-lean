# ClassicalTheorems

- **Gödel’s Incompleteness Theorem:** Every satisfiable recursively enumerable extension of the seven axioms of Robinson arithmetic Q has an independent sentence. The Gödel–Rosser proof combines bounded-witness and Rosser-comparison arguments with Foundation’s representation theorems for the standard natural numbers and its verified proof calculus. A semantic translation of the exact arithmetic symbols, with a primitive-recursive partial inverse, handles arbitrary enumerable axiom sets. The proof passes the standard-axiom audit, including the exclusion of a Church’s-thesis axiom.
- **The Independence of the Parallel Postulate:** The Euclidean real coordinate plane and the Klein open unit disk both satisfy Tarski’s neutral axioms 1–9 and full Dedekind continuity. The Euclidean plane satisfies axiom 10; the Klein disk refutes it. Hyperbolic segment congruence is defined by equality of normalized Lorentz inner products. All model axioms and the independence theorem pass the standard-axiom audit.
- **Euler’s Polyhedron Formula:** V − E + F = 2 for every full-dimensional convex polytope in ℝ³. Vertices, edges and facets are counted as nonempty exposed faces of affine dimensions 0, 1 and 2. A generic linear height orders the vertices, and local facet boundaries cancel the highest vertex of an edge cycle. Finite induction fills every cycle over 𝔽₂; the ranks of the geometric incidence matrices then give Euler’s formula. Supporting results include polygon, tetrahedron and pyramid formulas, incidence identities and the edge bound E ≤ 3V − 6.
- **Green’s Theorem:** The curl formula holds for a bounded connected Jordan domain with a positively oriented regular C¹ boundary and C¹ coefficients on a neighborhood of its closure. The proof uses MovingSofa’s Jordan-area identity, compact-support extensions and rank-one shears. Supporting results allow stationary boundary points, determine an orientation sign that works for all coefficient pairs, or infer the positive formula from one winding check. Signed, positive and absolute-value versions cover arbitrary nondegenerate compact parameter intervals, curves that are C¹ only near those intervals, and locally C¹ changes of parameter allowing pauses and retraced arcs. Finite piecewise boundaries allow corners and a separate local C¹ neighborhood for each piece; the boundary integral is the sum of the piece integrals. Certified vertex polygons, including collinear subdivision vertices, supply the Jordan geometry through Schönflies. Noncollinear triangles are a special case. All these results pass the standard-axiom audit.
- **The Isoperimetric Theorem:** For every continuous closed rectifiable planar Jordan parametrization on a nondegenerate compact interval, injective on the left half-open interval, 4π times the Lebesgue area of the interior is at most the square of the total-variation perimeter. Equality holds if and only if the boundary is a circle of positive radius. Continuity, closedness and simplicity determine the orientation; winding, convexity, radial uniqueness, speed and differentiability are not additional hypotheses. The orientation argument uses a Jordan–Schönflies homeomorphism, integer-circle deformations and homotopy invariance of winding. The analytic bound and circle rigidity use Fourier estimates for absolutely continuous curves, the Jordan-area identity, exact reparametrization by variation and an almost-everywhere rotation ODE. Affine normalization handles arbitrary compact intervals. Supporting results include exact area and perimeter formulas for circles. [proofs.md](proofs.md) explains these steps.
- **Heron’s Formula:** For any three noncollinear vertices in the Euclidean plane, the Lebesgue area enclosed by their three side segments equals the square root of the semiperimeter product. The area is derived geometrically rather than supplied as a trigonometric-area hypothesis.
- **Pascal’s Hexagon Theorem:** Six homogeneous points on a conic defined by a nonzero quadratic form have collinear opposite-side intersections. A second declaration uses a nonsingular symmetric matrix to describe the conic and implies the first formulation. The homogeneous formulation requires no distinctness assumption. This translation of the HOL Light conic and bracket argument uses a Veronese determinant identity checked by Lean’s kernel.
- **Desargues’s Theorem:** Over any field, concurrency of the lines through corresponding vertices implies that the three corresponding side intersections lie on a nonzero common line. A second declaration proves the converse for noncollinear triangles. The coordinate proof uses a determinant identity checked by Lean’s kernel. Nonzero representatives and intersections give the usual interpretation in projective points.
